WHAT THIS IS. Each document is queried by its own title, twice, against the same
stored vectors: once BARE, once with a query-side instruct prefix. Paired, so each
doc is its own control and corpus composition cannot explain a difference.
n=15 per band, seed 7, k=10.

⚠️ SCOPE — carry this label wherever the numbers are quoted.

  1. It measures THIS TASK STRING, not "the effect of prefixing":

       "Instruct: Given a search query, retrieve relevant documents that match
        the query\nQuery: {q}"

     One fixed module-level string, identical across all five bands, so prefix
     and task-wording are not varying together. That was the right design for
     isolating prefix-vs-bare, and it is exactly why the result does not transfer
     to a different string. `groton` measured 5/10 generic vs 7/10 domain-specific
     on their own corpus — the better one with the worse tail (a query fell rank
     2 -> rank 9). The string is a live variable.

  2. Querying a document by its own title makes the correct answer true by
     construction. That removes selection bias and it also removes headroom where
     `bare` is already high. READ `bare` BEFORE READING THE DELTA — where bare is
     near-ceiling, a null means "no power", not "no effect". Top-5 at 0-200 is the
     clearest case here: 14/15 bare leaves almost nothing to gain.

  3. n=15 per band. Adequate for the large short-band gains; NOT adequate for
     establishing "no harm" anywhere. The 2000+ band is re-run separately at n=120
     (instruct-prefix-2000plus-2026-08-02.txt) because that is the band where bare
     qwen3 already BEATS 3-large (42.4% vs 18.8%) and therefore the band the prefix
     could plausibly hurt.

⭐ READ THE DISCORDANT PAIRS, NOT THE NET. Two bands prove why:

  band        n   bare r1  inst r1   improved  worsened   net
  0-200      15      8       11          4        1       +3
  200-500    15      6       11          5        0       +5
  500-1000   15      9       15          6        0       +6
  1000-2000  15     10       10          1        1       +0   <-- a net ZERO that
  2000+      15      4        6          2        0       +2       is NOT inert
  TOTAL      75     37       53         18        2      +16

  top-5: 56/75 -> 63/75. Unchanged in three of five bands (0-200, 1000-2000, 2000+).

⛔ THE PREFIX IS NOT MONOTONE IN DOCUMENT LENGTH. The pilot's "7 changed, ALL 7
improved" held at 200-500 and does not generalise. Two queries were BROKEN:

  0-200      "Inkling — Landing Page and UI Decisions"
             rank-1 bare -> ABSENT from top-10 under the prefix (the worst form:
             not demoted, gone)
  1000-2000  "quantum-feed distributed-build: CELL-WRITE LEVER GATE-#1 GRE..."
             rank-1 bare -> rank 1 under the prefix

⇒ The effect turns at BOTH ends of the range measured here, so extrapolating
either upward or downward from a measured band is unsafe. This matters for other
seats: `groton` has 7,432 chunks below 200 tokens — beneath every band measured
here — and `mind` sits at 249-386 tok/row.

PRE-REGISTRATION, HONOURED. Before running, I predicted the prefix would help
LITTLE — the published effect is 1-5% and the gap to close was ~33 points. Measured
+21.4 points of rank-1 overall (49.3% -> 70.7%), and +33 points in the pilot band.
I WAS WRONG BY AN ORDER OF MAGNITUDE. Recording it because a pre-registration that
survives being wrong is the only kind that shows the number wasn't chosen after
seeing the data.

CONTEXT — the census this is diagnosing (3-large -> BARE qwen3, n=7,221, document
path, same input text): 0-200 78.5->61.8 · 200-500 76.9->43.4 · 500-1000 76.6->50.8
· 1000-2000 51.8->48.6 · 2000+ 18.8->42.4.

⛔ NOT YET A FIX. memo has no chokepoint: 25 call sites share one embeddings.embed(),
with the query/document distinction living only in local variable names. Applying
this needs embed_query()/embed_document() with the ambiguous one REMOVED, and ~21
call sites reclassified by hand.
